Two lamps can look almost identical when they are switched off and feel completely different when they are on.

The reason is often the bulb.

Color temperature, brightness, color rendering and dimming all affect whether a room feels warm and comfortable or bright and clinical. The numbers on the box can look technical, but you only need to understand a few of them to make a better choice.

## The Short Answer: 2700K or 3000K?

- **Choose 2700K** for a softer, warmer evening atmosphere in bedrooms, living rooms and quiet corners.
- **Choose 3000K** when you still want warm light but need slightly clearer visibility for reading, getting dressed or mixed-use spaces.

Neither is universally better. The right choice depends on what you do in the room and how the bulb looks through the shade.

## What Does Kelvin Mean?

Kelvin, shown as **K**, describes the appearance of the light.

Lower numbers look warmer and more golden. Higher numbers look cooler and more blue-white.

A simple household guide:

- **2200K-2400K:** very warm, amber and candle-like
- **2700K:** traditional warm household light
- **3000K:** warm but cleaner and slightly less golden
- **4000K:** neutral to cool white
- **5000K and above:** bright daylight-like appearance

For a Soft Moon Studio-style evening space, 2700K is usually the safest starting point.

## When 2700K Works Best

Use 2700K when the main purpose is winding down.

It suits:

- bedside lamps
- living-room table lamps
- paper lanterns
- shaded floor lamps
- reading corners used mainly at night
- accent lights on shelves

Warm light tends to complement wood, linen, cream, terracotta and other soft natural colors. It can also make a room feel more intimate because the light is less crisp.

## When 3000K Works Best

Choose 3000K when 2700K feels too yellow or when the space needs more clarity.

It can work well for:

- a reading lamp beside a chair
- a bedroom that is also used as a workspace
- a dressing area
- a kitchen or dining area with warm finishes
- a room that receives cooler daylight during the day

The light still reads as warm, but white paper and pale surfaces usually look a little cleaner.

## Lumens Tell You How Bright the Bulb Is

Watts describe energy use. **Lumens** describe brightness.

As a rough starting point:

- **200-450 lumens:** accent light or a very small lamp
- **450-800 lumens:** common table-lamp range
- **800-1100 lumens:** brighter task or general lighting

The shade changes how much light reaches the room. A dark or tightly woven shade may need more lumens than a pale linen or paper shade.

For a cozy result, do not automatically choose the brightest bulb the fixture allows. Use several moderate light sources instead of making one lamp do all the work.

## CRI Helps Colors Look Natural

CRI stands for Color Rendering Index. It indicates how naturally colors appear under a light source.

A bulb with **90+ CRI** is a useful choice when you care about skin tones, art, wood and textiles looking natural. This can make a subtle but noticeable difference in a carefully styled room.

CRI does not tell you whether the bulb is warm or cool. Check CRI and kelvin separately.

## Dimmable Does Not Always Mean Compatible

If your lamp or wall switch uses a dimmer, choose a bulb specifically marked dimmable. Even then, some bulb and dimmer combinations may flicker, hum or have a limited dimming range.

Check:

- whether the fixture has a built-in dimmer
- whether the bulb is labeled dimmable
- whether the bulb manufacturer lists compatible dimmer types
- the fixture's maximum wattage and bulb base

For lamps without a built-in dimmer, a smart bulb can offer app or voice control, but only if that extra technology genuinely makes the room easier to use.

## Do Shades Change the Color?

Yes. A cream linen shade can make a 3000K bulb appear warmer. Frosted glass diffuses glare. A colored or very dense shade may tint the light or reduce brightness.

This is why it is useful to test one bulb in the actual lamp before replacing every bulb in the room.

## Keep Color Temperature Consistent

Several lamps in one room usually feel calmer when their color temperatures are close. A 2700K table lamp beside a cool-white ceiling light can make the difference feel more abrupt.

The bulbs do not need to match perfectly, especially when one is used for a task. But keeping the main evening lights within the 2700K-3000K range creates a more coherent atmosphere.

## A Simple Choice for Most Homes

Start with a dimmable 2700K, 90+ CRI LED bulb in the lamp you use most at night. If it feels too golden for reading or detailed tasks, try 3000K in that specific task lamp.
